# Going Up!

A lift game for young kids. The front of a tall city building has been cut away so you can see every floor: the
lifts run up the middle, with rooms on both sides (homes with living rooms, kitchens and kids' bedrooms, offices,
a hotel, a café, a restaurant, public toilets, a pool, a playroom, a library, a roof garden with a playground or an
observatory), and under the street an underground car park (on some levels two, with a bike room or a laundry) and a
subway station. Each level is its own kind of building: a block of flats, an office tower, a hotel or a mixed tower.
A building can have several floors of the same kind; each has its own colour (for example blue and pink offices, or
teal, light blue and caramel homes, the last one with a cat).
People walk in from the street, get out of their cars, step off subway trains or come out of the rooms, wait by the
lifts and show a bubble with the floor they want (its colour, a little picture and a big number). Floors are numbered
like a real lift: the lobby at street level is 0, the floors above are 1, 2, 3…, and the ones underground are -1, -2, -3. Tap a floor to send a lift there. Get everyone where they're going before they get
cross and stomp off down the stairs.

Play it at <https://tb-x.github.io/going-up/> (once published).

## How it plays

- Tap a floor in a lift shaft and that lift goes there. Tap a floor in the rooms and the nearest free lift goes.
- People wait for the lift on whichever side of the lifts they come from (there's a queue on each side). The doors
  open by themselves: people for that floor step out and cheer, then the people waiting on both sides get in, the
  one who has waited longest first.
- A moving lift stops on the way wherever someone inside wants to get out, and a lift standing still opens for anyone
  waiting on its floor.
- The lamps on top of a lift show how full it is. When it's full the lamps flash and the next person waits.
- Faces go from happy to grumpy to red and steaming the longer people wait. Waiting inside a lift is much less
  annoying. A red, steaming person gives up and takes the stairs (counted next to the walking man at the top).
- Each level needs a number of happy arrivals (the little people at the top fill in green). Three stars if nobody
  took the stairs, two if one or two did, otherwise one. There's no losing and no timer.
- Fourteen levels, each its own building (floors listed from the bottom, lobby = 0):
  1. Corner flats: lobby, café, homes.
  2. Small apartment block: lobby, café, two home floors.
  3. Apartment block at night: lobby, three home floors, roof garden with a playground.
  4. Small office tower: car park, lobby, café, two office floors (toilets on one).
  5. Hotel at dusk: car park, lobby with toilets, restaurant, two hotel floors, pool.
  6. Apartment block at night: two car parks (-2 with a bike room, -1), lobby, café, three home floors.
  7. Office tower: car park, lobby, offices with toilets, restaurant, offices, pool, roof garden.
  8. Office tower at dusk: subway, car park, lobby with toilets, restaurant, two office floors, pool.
  9. Big hotel at night: subway, car park, lobby with toilets, restaurant, two hotel floors, pool, roof garden
     (morning rush).
  10. Apartment tower: two car parks (-2 with a laundry), lobby, café, three home floors, roof garden with a playground.
  11. Mixed tower at dusk: subway, car park, lobby, café, offices, library, two home floors, observatory.
  12. Apartment tower at night: subway (-3), two car parks (-2 with a bike room), lobby, café, homes, homes,
      playroom, homes, roof garden with a playground.
  13. Offices and hotel: subway, car park, lobby, café, offices with toilets, offices, restaurant, two hotel floors,
      roof garden (lunch rush).
  14. Mixed tower at night: subway, two car parks (-2 with a laundry), lobby with toilets, café, offices, three home
      floors, roof garden with a playground (morning and lunch rush).

  A subway train pulls in every 20-30 seconds and a few people get off at once. Cars drive down the street ramp into
  the upper car park (-1); cars for the lower one (-2) carry on down its inside ramp. Later levels add a second and
  third lift, slow grandmas, impatient pizza couriers and dog walkers (the dog takes a seat too).
- Who goes where depends on the building and the time of day: by day people leave their homes for the subway or the
  car park and come in from the street to the offices; at dusk they leave work; at night they head home, to the hotel
  and to the restaurant.
- People only come and go through real openings: the lobby entrance, room doors, the stair door, car doors and
  train doors. Every trip is shown: people stroll in along the pavement and through the lobby door, drive into the car park and
  climb out of their car, or step off a subway train. After the lift they walk on: out to the street, into a room,
  back to a parked car that then drives away, or to the platform to wait for the next train (which comes sooner when
  someone is waiting). Only waiting for a lift makes people cross; walking, driving and waiting for a train don't.
- Some levels are at dusk or at night (a sun, sunset or moon on each level button), about every third level. The
  win at night ends with "Good night!".
- The rooms are busy: in the toilets the cubicle doors close (red lock, feet under the door) and open again (green),
  people wash their hands and use the dryer, and now and then a cleaner mops the floor behind a yellow wet-floor sign.
- The people in the rooms are the building's own people. Someone heading for the lift gets up from their chair (a
  diner, an office worker, a hotel guest, someone at home) and walks out to the lift; someone arriving walks to an
  empty seat and sits back down. Now and then (about 1 in 6) they're only off to the toilets: they ride there, go in
  (a cubicle locks red), come out 20-40 seconds later and ride back to their own seat.
- The toilets are a real room: people walk in from the lane, go into a free cubicle (the door shuts, the lock turns
  red and their feet show) or to a urinal, wash their hands, use the dryer and walk out again. Nobody appears or vanishes
  in there; a cubicle is only busy while someone is inside. Now and then someone from the same floor pops in too, and a
  cleaner comes in from the lift side with her cart, mops behind a wet-floor sign and leaves the same way.
- The restaurant runs for real: a diner raises a hand, the waiter takes the order and carries it across to the kitchen
  pass, the cook fetches from the fridge, fries, plates up and puts the plate on the pass, the waiter serves it, the food
  gets smaller as they eat, and the empty plates go back to the kitchen to be washed and racked.
- In the pool a child swims lengths (front crawl, splashes, turning at each end) while the lifeguard watches, and in the
  paddling pool a child sits and splashes next to a bobbing rubber ring.
- Little animals live around the building: a mole digs through the soil under the street and pushes up molehills, a cat
  roams the pavement (onto the bench, a bin, over the zebra to a window ledge), a dog sniffs the trees on its walk,
  crows hop on the roof railing and pigeons sit on the street lamps, and a squirrel runs up and down a street tree.
- In a building with a hotel, the lobby desk is a hotel reception (a rack of keys, a key sign, a luggage trolley).
  Hotel guests come in from the street with a little suitcase, wait at the desk for their key, then queue for the lift.
- The two hotel floors are full of life. The first has a guest room (big bed, wardrobe, desk with a TV, armchair, a guest
  unpacking her suitcase, the housekeeper pushing her cart, making the bed and vacuuming) and the hotel's own reception
  and lounge (a curved desk with a receptionist, key rack, bell and a bed sign, a concierge, a luggage trolley, sofa and
  armchairs). Everyone who arrives on that floor stops at the desk for a key before going on. The second hotel floor has
  a breakfast room (buffet, coffee station, guests eating, a waiter with a tray) and a suite (someone reading in bed,
  a sofa corner, the housekeeper doing her round).
- On level 1 a pulsing hand shows what to tap if nothing has been tapped for a couple of seconds (later levels: 8 s).
- When the building is taller than the screen, drag up and down to look around (down into the basements too);
  on a narrow phone the outer rooms may be cut off, so drag sideways as well. The lifts and both queues always stay
  on screen. Arrows at the edge show (with a face) that someone is waiting out of view. Tap an arrow to go there.
- Sounds are synthesized in the browser (Web Audio), and the spoken words use the browser's own voice. The speaker
  button turns them off and is remembered.

## Running it

Serve the folder and open it in a browser:

```
python -m http.server 5224
```

then go to <http://localhost:5224/>. Opening `index.html` straight from disk mostly works too.

Handy URL options: `?level=3` starts level 3 straight away, `?demo` lets the computer play, `?fast` runs four
times faster, `?mute` keeps it completely silent, `?seed=7` makes the arrivals repeatable.

Stars are kept in the browser (`goingup.stars`), as is the sound setting (`goingup.muted`).

## Tuning knobs

At the top of the script in `index.html`:

| Name | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CAR_SPEED` | 5.5 | lift top speed (a floor is 3 units) |
| `CAR_ACCEL` | 9 | how quickly a lift speeds up and slows down |
| `DOOR_TIME` | 0.45 s | doors opening or closing |
| `MAX_DPR_PHONE` / `MAX_DPR` | 1.5 / 2 | sharpness cap on phones / bigger screens (lower = faster, a little softer) |
| `DWELL_MIN` | 0.9 s | doors stay open at least this long |
| `BOARD_GAP` | 0.3 s | gap between two people stepping in |
| `WALK_SPEED` | 1.6 | how fast people walk |
| `QUEUE_MAX` | 5 | most people waiting on one floor |
| `MAX_PEOPLE` | 30 | most people in the building |
| `RIDE_PATIENCE_RATE` | 0.3 | patience runs out this much slower inside a lift |
| `MOOD_T` | 0.35, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 | share of patience used up before each mood step (the last one means the stairs) |
| `HINT_DELAY_L1` / `HINT_DELAY` | 2 s / 8 s | idle time before the hand hint shows |
| `MIN_FLOOR_PX` | 64 | a floor is never drawn shorter than this; taller buildings scroll |
| `CELEBRATE_TIME` | 1.2 s | how long delivered people cheer |
| `SPECIAL_CHANCE` | 12% | chance of each special person (grandma, courier, dog walker) once a level has them |
| `ARRIVE_JITTER` | 30% | randomness in the time between arrivals |
| `TRIP_MIX` | 40%, 35% | lobby to upstairs, upstairs going down; the rest go between upper floors |
| `PARK_SHARE` | 18% | share of arrivals that come out of a parked car (levels with a car park) |
| `DOWN_TO_BASEMENT` | 40% | how often people going down want the car park or subway instead of the lobby |
| `TRAIN_EVERY` / `TRAIN_GROUP` | 20-30 s / 2-4 | how often a subway train arrives, and how many get off |
| `TRAIN_DOOR_DELAY` | 1.4 s | train stops and opens before people step out |
| `QUEUE_ROOM` | 4.2 | width each side of the lifts that is always kept on screen for the queues |
| `TRAIN_GAP_WAITING` | 8 s | the next train comes within this time when someone waits on the platform |
| `TRAIN_OPEN_MIN` / `TRAIN_OPEN_MAX` | 2 s / 6 s | how long the train doors stay open |
| `PARKED_AT_START` | 1-3 | cars already parked when a level starts |
| `CAR_DOOR_TIME` | 0.6 s | how long a car door stays open while someone climbs out |
| `NIGHT_WEIGHTS` | | how much more (or less) popular each kind of floor is as a destination by day, at dusk and at night |
| `ORIGIN_WEIGHTS` | | which kinds of floor trips start from, by time of day (homes by day, offices at dusk) |
| `HOME_TO_BASEMENT` | 70% | by day, how often people leaving a home floor want the subway or the car park |
| `LOWER_PARK_SHARE` | 50% | with two car parks, the share of car-park arrivals that use the lower one (-2) |
| `LEVEL_TIMES` | | day, dusk or night for each level |
| `ROOM_DOOR_Z`, `STAIR_DOOR_Z`, `PARK_DOOR_Z`, `TRAIN_EDGE_Z`, `PAVEMENT_Z` | | where people stand when they go through a room door, the stair door, into a car or a train, and the line they walk along the pavement |
| `LOO_CHANCE` / `LOO_TIME` | 1 in 6 / 20-40 s | how often someone getting up goes to the toilets instead (levels with toilets), and how long they stay |
| `LOO_EVERY` / `LOO_AMBIENT_MAX` | 15-30 s / 2 | how often someone from the toilets floor itself pops in, and how many at once |
| `ART_REST_EAT` / `ART_REST_IDLE` | 18 s / 10-25 s | (art) how long a plate lasts, and the pause between a table's meals |
| `CHECKIN_TIME` | 3 s | how long a hotel guest waits at a reception desk (the lobby's, or the hotel floor's) |
| `FAST_MULT` | 4 | speed-up for `?fast` |
| `BUBBLE_PX` / `RIDER_BUBBLE` | 44 px / 0.7 | bubble size on screen, and how much smaller they are inside a lift |

The `LEVELS` list sets, per level: the floors from bottom to top (from `THEME_LIB`), lifts, seats per lift, seconds between arrivals, patience (seconds),
how many arrivals win, which special people appear, which rushes happen, and `wings`, which swaps a wing's room for
toilets, a bike room, a laundry or a playground (for example `wings: { office: { R: 'toilets' } }`). `THEME_LIB` has
variants of some floors (`home2`, `home3`, `office2`, `hotel2`, `parking2`): the same kind of room with its own colour,
icon, furniture and people.
